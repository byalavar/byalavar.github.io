DIAL: Label-Free Mitigation of Spurious Correlations in VLMs Using Sparse Autoencoders

B. C. Yalavarthi, N. Ratha, V. Govindaraju · ICLR 2026

Paper Code Poster

The problem

CLIP-style models often predict from features that are correlated with the label but not causal for it. The model looks at the water background instead of the bird, the imaging artifact instead of the lesion, or the gender instead of the hair color. These shortcuts fail exactly on the groups where reliability matters. Most existing fixes need retraining, labels, extra data, or an LLM in the loop, which takes away the zero-shot advantage these models are used for.

The method

DIAL removes the shortcut directly from the image embeddings, in three steps.

  1. Disentangle. A pretrained sparse autoencoder maps the embeddings into a sparse feature basis where individual directions are interpretable.
  2. Identify. Each feature gets a score based on how strongly it fires on samples with the spurious attribute and how well its direction aligns with the attribute text embedding. The top scoring features span the spurious subspace.
  3. Remove. The spurious subspace is projected out of the embeddings. Only samples flagged as likely biased are modified, so clean samples stay untouched.

DIAL+ removes the last requirement. It detects the spurious attributes on its own, so the method needs nothing beyond the embeddings.

Results

Across CelebA, Waterbirds, FMOW, ISIC and COVID-19, DIAL matches or beats baselines that rely on labels, extra data or LLMs, with consistent gains in worst-group accuracy. Two choices matter for this: projecting out the whole subspace works better than zeroing individual features, and intervening only on flagged samples works better than intervening everywhere. The method also makes retrieval fairer, reducing MaxSkew@1000 on FairFace across age, gender and ethnicity. And since every removed feature has a name, the whole intervention can be inspected.
